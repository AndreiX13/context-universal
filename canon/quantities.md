# Canon — Quantities and material lists

> Owner rule, 22–23 Nov 2026: "never on assumptions"; "extra attention to materials, when we make the
> lists, to everything. Not a single mistake, everything precise, with a check for this kind of list,
> so we don't wake up later with errors." Applies to every project: offers, cost estimates, material
> lists, supplier requests, progress statements (situații de lucrări), contracts.

## Where a quantity may come from

- **Measured by us on the drawing or the model**, with the sheet and the method written next to it:
  "IS01, 4 channel symbols × 4.0 m measured at 1:500", "DWG, polyline length on layer X".
- **Product data** from the manufacturer's own document (catalogue, data sheet), with its date.
- **The owner's figure or decision.**

Nothing else is a source. The designer's bill of quantities, a colleague's offer, a previous sheet of
ours, a search snippet: each of these had errors in the first real case where we checked
(see the lessons below). They are useful only as a list of things to look for on the drawing.

## How to measure

- **Count symbols, measure lines at scale, read levels.** Every line of the list is traceable to a
  place on a sheet.
- **No hidden multipliers.** If something is added for waste, joints or connections, it is its own
  figure with its reason, not a percentage folded into the quantity.
- **Verticals come from heights** (sections, schemas, levels). If the drawings don't give the height,
  that quantity is not measured yet: name it and ask.
- **What the drawing doesn't show** (no dimensions, no terrain levels, no roof structure) is listed
  as a question for the client before the offer, never filled with a guess.
- **A new rule applies backwards.** When a rule like this one appears, every figure already in the
  work in progress is re-checked, not only the new ones.

## The check before a list leaves the house

1. **Second, independent count.** A different method (PDF vectors vs. the CAD model, symbols vs.
   schema) or a separate pass that has not seen the first figures.
2. **Compare line by line.** Counts must match exactly; for lengths, every difference is explained on
   the drawing. Nothing ships with an unexplained difference.
3. **Concordance across documents.** The offer, the price list, the contract, the supplier requests
   and the measurement sheet share each figure; a script or a table proves they agree.
4. **Keep the measurement sheet** (internal), so any later question ("why 16 m?") is answered from the
   sheet, not from memory.
5. **Say what was checked and what wasn't** when handing the list over. A list with an open item
   says so on the item.

## Why this is a rule, not a preference

A signed contract freezes the quantities. A missing item or a wrong length found on site is paid by
us, and found in the office it costs nothing. Every check costs less than the cheapest mistake.

## Lessons — an installation offer, Nov 2026 **[RO]**

Mistakes found late in one of the owner's offers, all from figures taken ready-made:
- the drainage channels: 4 m in the designer's list, 16 m on the site plan (4 × 4 m);
- 27 of 70 quantities copied from the designer's lists, which were known to be wrong (bathtubs and
  showers not on the sheets, 2,166 m of insulation, fire penetrations copied from another building);
- the old CAD file in the project folder, the new one only in a temporary folder;
- plans without dimensions sent to a reviewer for a cost estimate he could not make from them.
